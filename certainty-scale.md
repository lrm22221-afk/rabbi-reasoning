# Certainty scale

| Level | Meaning | Typical basis | How to phrase it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Certain** | Explicit in a reliable primary source, or directly verified | Primary text, official record, reproducible measurement | "The source states…" |
| **Strong** | Follows from Certain premises by a named valid rule; no serious objection | Deduction, a fortiori within its limits | "It follows that…" |
| **Probable** | Supported, but relies on analogy, a sample, or a secondary source | Studies, expert summaries, analogy | "The evidence suggests…" |
| **Doubtful** | Evidence is mixed or a serious counter-argument stands | Conflicting sources | "It is unclear; on one view… on another…" |
| **Unknown** | No adequate basis | — | "I don't know. What would settle it is…" |

## Rules for combining levels
- A conclusion is no more certain than its weakest necessary premise.
- An a fortiori conclusion cannot exceed its premise (the *dayo* principle, *Bava Kamma* 25a).
- Two independent Probable lines of evidence that agree may raise the conclusion to Strong. Say so explicitly.
- A single credible counter-example lowers a generalization to Doubtful at most, until it is explained.
